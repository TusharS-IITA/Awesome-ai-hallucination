# Curated Datasets for Hallucination and Factuality Evaluation

- **TruthfulQA**
  **Source:** Lin, S., Hilton, J., & Evans, O. (2022)
  **Description:** A benchmark dataset containing 817 questions across 38 categories designed to measure whether a language model generates truthful answers or confidently mimics common human falsehoods and misconceptions.
  **Application:** Evaluating the baseline factuality and calibration (whether the model knows what it doesn't know) of foundational language models.
  [View Dataset](https://github.com/sylinrl/TruthfulQA)

- **HaluEval (Hallucination Evaluation Benchmark)**
  **Source:** Li, J., Cheng, X., Zhao, W. X., et al. (2023)
  **Description:** A large-scale collection of 5,000 general user queries and 30,000 task-specific examples (QA, dialogue, summarization) containing both ground-truth and human-annotated hallucinated samples generated by LLMs.
  **Application:** Training and evaluating uncertainty-aware models on their ability to recognize, classify, and filter different types of factual hallucinations in generated text.
  [View Dataset](https://github.com/RUCAIBox/HaluEval)

- **SelfCheckGPT WikiBio Dataset**
  **Source:** Manakul, P., Liusie, A., & Gales, M. J. F. (2023)
  **Description:** A specialized dataset of Wikipedia biographies generated by GPT-3, manually annotated at the sentence level for factual accuracy, identifying both major and minor hallucinations. 
  **Application:** Testing zero-resource hallucination detection methods and evaluating semantic uncertainty metrics that rely on measuring disagreement across multiple model responses.
  [View Dataset](https://github.com/potsawee/selfcheckgpt)
